### 5.1 The chapter argument
- **EN (p11):** "AI maturity is not the number of pilots, licenses, or people who attended prompt training. It is the demonstrated ability to operate AI at a declared level of authority, consequence, and dependency. An organization can be mature in document search and immature in credit decisions. ### 5.4 Exit gate (p12 EN / p13 TH)
- **EN:** "A workflow should advance only when it passes an exit gate. Moving from Assist to Manage requires representative cases, baseline comparison, an owner, reviewer guidance, and rollback. ## 6. Framework attributions — exactly what each source does and does not support

**[3] ISO/IEC 42001 → "documented level".** The standard supports the *management-system* framing: an organization establishes, implements, maintains and continually improves an AI management system; policy, objectives, responsibility, lifecycle processes, monitoring and continual improvement are documented and auditable. That is what makes "the documented level" a real artifact against which real permissions can be compared — the mechanism behind authority debt.
**It does NOT support:** any maturity ladder (ISO/IEC 42001 defines none), any claim that certification demonstrates maturity, or any reproduction of the standard's requirements. Carry the book's Boundary line.

**[4] March (1991) → why "advance every workflow" is the wrong default.** The paper models the trade-off between exploring new possibilities and exploiting old certainties, and shows that adaptive processes which refine exploitation faster than exploration become "effective in the short run but self-destructive in the long run."
**It does NOT say anything about AI, about autonomy, or about maturity levels.** The bridge — *pushing every workflow up the ladder is an exploitation monoculture that starves the organisation of the exploration its next gate depends on* — is the **author's mapping**, and the prose must say so. Carry the book's Boundary line: a theoretical model illuminates a design problem; it does not prescribe a universal portfolio allocation.

**[2] Masterclass → the tool→AI-first path.** Supports only that the episode presents a five-step progression from AI as a personal tool to an AI-first organisation, and that it warns against confusing tool ownership with organisational change. Paraphrase only; label as practitioner synthesis, not a study.

**[1] The book → everything else in this post.** Label `Synthesis`. The five levels carry Q1 verbatim.
