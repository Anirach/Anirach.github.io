### 0.7 The five operating principles (printed p. 44, verbatim, never renumbered)

1. **Maintain a live inventory and named owner** Governance begins with knowing what exists.
2. **Map obligations to evidence** Reuse artifacts responsibly without claiming frameworks are identical.
3. **Use law as the floor and ethics for the gap** Define prohibited uses affected groups and remedy.
4. **Make supplier change and exit governable** Vendor dependence is part of the risk perimeter.
5. **Measure lifecycle sustainability and rebound** Unit efficiency can coexist with rising total impact.

### 0.13 Appendix D.1 — the four evidence labels (printed p. 91, verbatim)

- **Standard or law | มาตรฐานหรือกฎหมาย.** A normative requirement, official legal text, or public description of a standard. A voluntary framework is never presented as law.
- **Study | งานวิจัย.** A peer-reviewed article or well-identified working paper with a declared sample and method. Findings remain tied to that task, population, model generation, and time.
- **Field signal | สัญญาณจากภาคปฏิบัติ.** A survey, institutional report, case synthesis, or monitoring resource. It can guide questions and priorities but usually cannot establish causality.
- **Author synthesis | การสังเคราะห์ของผู้เขียน.** A framework built by connecting engineering, organization, workforce, and governance evidence. It is offered as a testable design, not as settled fact.

**"A voluntary framework is never presented as law" is the sentence that governs this post's reference labels.** NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 42005, the OECD Principles, the two ASEAN guides and both ETDA guidelines are `Standard`, never `Law`. Only the PDPA, the EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the PDPC sub-regulations index and the ETDA AI-law status page are `Law`.
