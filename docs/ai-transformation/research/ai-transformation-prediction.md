## Verbatim material the writer may use

### The p4 chain — "The two nonnegotiable distinctions" (printed p4)
> **Prediction is not decision.** A forecast estimates what may happen. Judgment selects objectives, weighs consequences, applies values, and owns responsibility. The complete chain is data to estimate to judgment to action to outcome to feedback.
>
> **Proposal is not effect.** A model may propose a tool and arguments. An external guard must independently check identity, authority, schema, parameters, risk, approval, transaction limits, and post-state before an effect is accepted as real.

⚠️ **Chain-wording collision — resolve it once and keep it consistent across both tracks.** The book states the chain three different ways:
| Where | Chain |
|---|---|
| p4 (the spec's chain table) | data → **estimate** → judgment → action → outcome → **feedback** |
| App A §5 | ข้อมูล → **การคาดการณ์** → ดุลยพินิจ → **การกระทำ** → ผลลัพธ์ (no feedback) |
| Figure 1 | DATA ข้อมูล → CONTEXT บริบท → INTELLIGENCE ปัญญา → JUDGMENT ดุลยพินิจ → ACTION **การลงมือ** → OUTCOME ผลลัพธ์ → LEARNING การเรียนรู้ |
| Glossary #2 | "การลงมือทำ" for action |
**Ruling for this post:** the §3 chain table follows **p4** (six elements, ending in feedback); the §6 worksheet's Element column follows the spec's own list (Prediction / Judgment / Action / Outcome / Feedback). Say once, in prose, that the book writes "estimate" where the worksheet writes "prediction" and that they name the same step. ### App A §4 — cheaper prediction as the economic lens (source for §4's opening)
Verbatim (EN): *"Instead of asking only what AI can demonstrate, the episode asks which input is becoming cheaper. Its economic answer is prediction: using available information to estimate unknown information. This includes demand, risk, failure, next-best action, document content, or likely response, not only a numerical forecast."* … *"Electricity did more than replace individual power sources; it enabled new factory and city designs."* … *"But installing AI inside a process designed for scarce analysis captures only a fraction of the value."*

### App A §5 — separate prediction, judgment, action, outcome (source for §3)
Verbatim (EN): *"Cheaper prediction does not make judgment disappear. … Data informs an estimate; judgment determines what the estimate means under objectives, constraints, values, and consequences; action changes the world; outcome reveals what actually happened. If leaders collapse prediction and judgment into one concept, they may expect a model to 'decide' without specifying priorities or tradeoffs. A system may estimate attrition risk, patient deterioration, fraud, or demand, but a responsible person or governed decision rule must still determine the intervention. As prediction becomes abundant, judgment can become more important because more estimates require interpretation.
